The Three Models
HAIKU 4.5
Fast · Cheap · Routine

Lightweight and fast. Handles tasks that don't require reasoning depth — formatting, retrieval, classification, simple generation. Think of this as the model that does the work when the answer is already in the room.

Workhorse · Balanced · Default

The production default. Strong reasoning, fast turnaround, and cost-efficient enough for sustained daily use. It's the right choice for 80% of professional knowledge work — the model you should feel guilty for not using.

The Four Effort Levels
Low

Minimal internal reasoning. Claude responds from pattern recognition — fast, cheap, direct. Adequate when the answer is deterministic or shallow.

Formatting, retrieval, simple Q&A, boilerplate
Medium

A meaningful reasoning budget. Claude thinks through multi-step problems, catches edge cases, and self-checks. The recommended default for most professional work.

Drafting, summarizing, structured analysis, light strategy
High

Significantly more thinking tokens. Claude considers tradeoffs, flags ambiguity, and produces more nuanced output. Best balance of quality and cost for complex tasks.

Strategy docs, discovery work, complex editing, product reasoning
Max

No ceiling on thinking budget. Deep, multi-step reasoning across long, complex problems. Noticeably slower and most expensive — use deliberately, not habitually.

Architecture decisions, hardest analysis problems, genuinely novel reasoning
The most common mistake: using Opus + Max as a "safe default" for everything. This is roughly 5–8× more expensive than Sonnet + Medium on identical tasks, with no quality difference on routine work. Effort level has more impact than model choice for most everyday tasks.

The decision framework in plain terms. Use this to reason about any task that doesn't appear in the grids.
Default to Sonnet + Medium
For anything you'd describe as "serious work," start here. Sonnet handles the vast majority of professional knowledge work. Medium gives Claude enough reasoning budget to catch errors and surface tradeoffs without the cost of deeper thinking. Only deviate with a reason.
Effort matters more than model for most tasks
Sonnet at High often outperforms Opus at Low for reasoning tasks. The model tier determines baseline capability; the effort level determines how deeply it applies that capability to your specific problem. Don't buy a more expensive model when the real gap is effort.
Upgrade to High when there's genuine ambiguity
If you've had to read the output twice and adjust it, that's usually a sign effort was too low — not that the model was wrong. High effort is where Claude flags tradeoffs and asks clarifying questions rather than committing to the most obvious interpretation.
Opus is not a safety net
The common pattern is: "I don't want to waste time, so I'll just use the best model." That's the wrong mental model. Opus is a specialist, not a generalist that's just better. It's tuned for agentic, long-horizon, genuinely novel problems — not "good strategy work."
Max effort is a tool for being stuck
Max is what you reach for when High didn't produce satisfying output on a genuinely hard problem. It's not a premium experience — it's extended compute for extended problems. If you're using it habitually, you're paying for thinking tokens that aren't doing more work.
The 80/20 check for your work
Most of your PI/RevOps work is Sonnet + Low/Med. Most of your H2 discovery work is Sonnet + High. Opus earns its cost only in: AI workforce strategy, genuinely novel product positioning decisions, or any session where you're synthesizing across 5+ conflicting frameworks simultaneously.
Quick heuristic: Before starting a task, ask — "Would a skilled analyst reach a conclusion on this in under 10 minutes with the right information?" If yes, that's Sonnet + Low or Med. If the analyst would need to sit with it, sketch it out, argue against their own view — that's High. If no analyst could do it in an hour without extensive domain expertise — that's Opus.
